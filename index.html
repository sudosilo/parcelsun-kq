What is this thing?

A parcel locker sitting in the desert sun bakes. This app shows how hot each side of the locker gets, how hot the screen glass gets, and how hot the air inside the screen console gets, hour by hour, for any day of the year.

Turn the locker to face a different direction, add shade, change the paint, or turn on an air conditioner, and see what each change does to the heat.

Why bother?

Picking where a locker goes and which way it faces costs almost nothing before install. Fixing heat damage after install costs a truck roll every time. This shows which placements will cook the screen and which ones stay workable.

How to read it

The 3D view shows the locker, the sun, and the shadows at the time on the slider. With Heat view on, each side is tinted by how hot its surface is: pale blue is cool, yellow is warm, red is very hot.

The chart shows the whole day. The dashed lines are your limits. Anything above them is a problem hour.

Okay, now the details

Sun position comes from the NOAA solar equations for the latitude and longitude you enter. Clear sky sunlight comes from the ASHRAE monthly clear sky model, split into direct beam, sky light, and light bounced off the ground.

Each outside face gets a sol air temperature: outside air plus the sunlight that face absorbs, divided by how fast wind carries heat off it. The roof also loses a little heat to the open night sky.

The console and the locker bays are two separate air spaces. Each one gains heat through its walls from its sol air temperatures, trades heat with the other through the shared side panel, and responds with a time lag you can set. The console also gets the heat from its electronics and screen.

Screen glass temperature balances the sun the glass absorbs and the outside air against the hot air behind it.

Outside air follows the average daily high and low for each month in the Las Vegas valley, with the low near dawn and the high mid afternoon. Heat wave add pushes the whole day hotter.

The awning shades the front from the top down based on how high the sun is and where it sits relative to the front. Full shade blocks direct sun on every face and part of the sky light. Indoor lobby removes the sun and uses the lobby air temperature.

All of this assumes clear sky. Clouds, dust storms, nearby walls, and parked cars are not modeled.
